About

Built by someone who got tired of asking.

WhyThis started from a small, recurring annoyance: opening a file, finding a line of code that made no obvious sense, and having no way to find out why it was there short of pinging someone and waiting. Most of the time the answer existed — in a closed pull request, a Slack thread, a Jira ticket — it just wasn't anywhere a person could actually search.

It's built and run by Sushma Varma, currently as a solo, early-stage effort rather than a funded team with a headcount to show for it. That's a deliberate starting point, not a limitation to apologize for: a small, focused build is easier to keep honest about what it does and doesn't do yet.

Why now

Retrieval-plus-synthesis — embedding a company's own history and asking a language model to answer strictly from it — only recently became reliable and cheap enough to build a product around. A plain keyword search over Slack or Jira never captured the connective tissue between a commit, the conversation around it, and the ticket that prompted it. That's the specific gap WhyThis is built to close, and no more than that.

How this gets built

A few working principles

01

Read-only, revocable, isolated

Every credential a client gives WhyThis can be read-only and revoked at any time. Every client's data is scoped so no other client can ever see it.

02

Honest about uncertainty

An answer either cites its source or says plainly that the indexed history doesn't cover it. A confident guess is worse than no answer.

03

Narrow before broad

One question — "why does this exist?" — answered well, before any temptation to become a general company chatbot or a wiki replacement.

04

Validate before scaling

Free pilots with real engineering teams come before paid rollout, and paid rollout comes before spending on growth.
